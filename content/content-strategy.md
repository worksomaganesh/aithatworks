# Content Strategy

## Editorial promise

**Less AI hype. More real-world results.**

Each video should help a defined viewer understand, evaluate, or implement a practical AI workflow. The problem is the story; the tool is a component.

## Starting content allocation

This is the first editorial hypothesis for launch. Review after the first 30 days and adjust using research and first-party analytics.

| Pillar | Allocation | Editorial job |
|---|---:|---|
| AI for business operations and implementations | 40% | Solve operational problems with end-to-end workflows |
| AI for founders and creators | 25% | Improve research, content, marketing, and solo-business systems |
| AI tools and practical experiments | 20% | Compare tools honestly against the same real task |
| AI-powered products and business systems | 10% | Build and evaluate useful products and repeatable systems |
| AI industry developments | 5% | Explain changes only when they alter practical decisions |

### Pillar 1 — Business operations and implementations
Examples:
- Enquiry intake, classification, routing, and follow-up.
- Lead qualification and CRM updates.
- Internal knowledge assistants grounded in approved documents.
- Customer-support triage with human escalation.
- Document extraction and approval workflows.
- Reporting and repetitive administrative processes.

The content must show system boundaries, permissions, failures, human handoffs, and how success is measured.

### Pillar 2 — Founders and creators
Examples:
- Research-to-content workflows.
- Content production operations.
- Competitor and market research assistants.
- Solo-founder operating systems.
- Marketing operations and campaign workflows.
- Scheduling, reporting, and repetitive task systems.

Do not turn this into generic prompt lists. Demonstrate the entire workflow and its real trade-offs.

### Pillar 3 — Tools and experiments
Examples:
- Compare two or more tools on the same task.
- Test a model's ability to handle a bounded workflow.
- Compare quality, latency, reliability, cost, and setup effort.
- Free versus paid tool evaluations.
- Test a new feature only when it changes a practical workflow.

Every comparison needs a fixed task, common inputs, criteria, test cases, and limitations.

### Pillar 4 — AI-powered products and systems
Examples:
- Build a small AI-powered utility around a validated repeated pain.
- Prototype a workflow product.
- Show product architecture and user journey.
- Test whether a repeated service task can become a reusable product.

Do not label a prototype as a launched SaaS or imply customers use it unless true.

### Pillar 5 — Industry developments
Examples:
- A model or platform change that materially changes a workflow.
- A pricing or API change that alters implementation economics.
- An AI policy or security change that affects deployment.
- A new capability tested against an existing task.

Do not publish generic launch summaries just to stay current.

## Signature video structure

1. **Problem:** Who has this problem, and what does it cost them in time, friction, or risk?
2. **Current process:** Show the existing workflow and its limitations.
3. **Decision:** Explain why AI is or is not appropriate.
4. **Build:** Show the components, tools, integrations, and boundaries.
5. **Demonstration:** Run real examples on approved test data.
6. **Evaluation:** Show failures, edge cases, human review, cost, and limitations.
7. **Result:** State what was measured and what remains unknown.
8. **Takeaway:** Who should use it, who should not, and what to do next?

The prototype and test should happen before the final script is written. This prevents narration from inventing capabilities or overstating results.

## Format mix

- **Short-form:** one problem, one workflow moment, one test result, or one decision. Do not cram an entire system into 30 seconds.
- **Long-form:** complete implementation, meaningful context, architecture, demonstration, evaluation, and limitations.
- **LinkedIn:** professional breakdowns, operating lessons, test evidence, and business implications.
- **Instagram:** concise demonstrations, useful visual breakdowns, and selected creator/founder workflows.

## Editorial distinction from other brands

AI That Works does not publish standalone programming tutorials, broad AI/ML fundamentals, cybersecurity curricula, or engineering career roadmaps. It may explain a technical concept only to make a particular implementation understandable.

No duplicate scripts or content splitting across TECHENIGMASTUDIOS and CONTXT AHEAD. Each brand must have its own question, evidence, story, and viewer payoff.

## Repurposing rules

Repurpose a tested implementation into platform-specific angles, not identical copies:
- Long video: complete workflow and evidence.
- Short 1: the problem and the surprising workflow step.
- Short 2: a test, failure, or useful result.
- LinkedIn: implementation breakdown and decision framework.
- Carousel: architecture, checklist, or lessons.

Do not invent a new claim while repurposing. Derivative assets must trace back to the same approved evidence record.
