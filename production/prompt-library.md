# AI That Works — Production Prompt Library

## Usage rules

- Replace bracketed inputs with approved project information.
- Attach source material and evidence where appropriate.
- Require the model to distinguish facts, estimates, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Do not allow generated scripts to invent test results, prices, integrations, or ROI.
- Save each prompt version and the outputs used in the episode production journal.
- Human review is required before an output becomes an approved asset.

## 1. Problem and opportunity researcher

Act as the research lead for AI That Works, an applied-AI media brand.

Investigate this workflow problem: [PROBLEM].

Target audience/context: [AUDIENCE AND BUSINESS CONTEXT].

Research the current process, repeated pain points, existing alternatives, evidence of demand, public practitioner discussions, available products, product documentation, implementation constraints, and likely risks.

Find what existing content already explains and identify a specific, evidence-backed gap in the coverage. Do not claim that a topic is underserved without inspecting relevant examples.

Separate verified evidence, attributed claims, analysis, estimates, and unknowns. Provide source URLs, publisher, publication date, access date, and the exact claim each source supports.

Finish with a preliminary opportunity score using the six-factor model in research/opportunity-system.md. Explain every rating and identify what must be validated before production.

## 2. AI-versus-non-AI workflow evaluator

Act as a workflow analyst. Determine whether [PROBLEM] should be addressed with AI, conventional automation, process redesign, software configuration, or a human-led process.

Map the current workflow, frequency, volume, exception rate, data quality, risks, cost, and user impact. Compare at least one non-AI alternative.

If AI is justified, define the narrowest suitable AI contribution, required integrations, permissions, human approval gates, failure handling, evaluation criteria, operational ownership, maintenance, and security controls.

Do not recommend AI merely because it is technically possible. State when it is not worth implementing.

## 3. Workflow architect and test planner

Design a bounded solution for [PROBLEM] for [TARGET BUSINESS/USER].

Available tools, budget, and constraints: [TOOLS AND CONSTRAINTS].

Produce:
1. Current-state workflow.
2. Proposed-state workflow.
3. Component and integration diagram description.
4. Data inputs, outputs, retention, and permissions.
5. Model/tool selection rationale and alternatives.
6. Human approval and escalation points.
7. Failure modes, retries, fallback, and recovery.
8. Security and privacy controls.
9. Actual costs to measure and estimated costs clearly labelled.
10. At least 10 realistic test cases including negative and edge cases.
11. Acceptance criteria and success metrics.
12. Deployment limitations and operational ownership.

Do not claim the system works until the test record confirms it.

## 4. Evidence-led scriptwriter

Turn this approved research, implementation, and test record into a video for AI That Works.

Topic: [TOPIC].
Primary audience: [AUDIENCE].
Problem: [PROBLEM].
Verified implementation state: [CONCEPT / CONTROLLED DEMO / TESTED / PILOT / OPERATIONAL].
Actual findings: [EVIDENCE].
Known limitations: [LIMITATIONS].

Produce five hooks, a recommended hook, a conversational long-form script, presenter segments, screen-recording instructions, diagram/B-roll directions, on-screen text, chapter structure, and an ending with a practical CTA.

Use only claims supported by the supplied evidence. Mark missing information as unknown. Do not invent money saved, time saved, accuracy, conversion, or ROI. Avoid generic AI hype and sales language.

## 5. Avatar presenter direction for Google Flow

Create a professional presenter sequence using the creator's own authorised avatar, @me, if the feature is available in the current account.

Preserve the creator's identity and natural audio likeness. Use a clean, modern, credible studio environment, consistent wardrobe/appearance, natural eye contact, restrained gestures, and conversational delivery.

Shot: [MEDIUM CLOSE-UP / CLOSE-UP / OTHER].
Aspect ratio: [16:9 / 9:16].
Presenter objective: [EXPLAIN / INTRODUCE / TRANSITION / CONCLUDE].
Approved script segment: [SCRIPT].

Avoid exaggerated expressions, unnecessary gestures, theatrical movement, visual artefacts, or background elements that distract from the information. Do not add words, claims, logos, or interface elements not present in the approved brief. Review generated speech and lip sync; do not assume perfect pronunciation.

## 6. Supporting B-roll / illustrative scene direction

Create a supporting visual for this exact narration point: [NARRATION].

Purpose of the shot: [EXPLAIN CONTEXT / SHOW AN ABSTRACT PROCESS / TRANSITION / EMPHASISE A RESULT].

Visual direction: [SCENE DESCRIPTION].
Required continuity: [COLOUR / ENVIRONMENT / OBJECTS / CAMERA].
Avoid: fake product UI, fabricated dashboards, misleading customer footage, text that must be factually exact, or a visual that implies an untested workflow is operational.

The scene is illustrative supporting footage, not evidence of a real product action. Keep important factual labels for verified overlays in post-production.

## 7. Diagram and motion designer

Create a diagram/motion plan for this workflow: [WORKFLOW].

Choose the semantic diagram type that best communicates the information: sequence, data flow, architecture, decision tree, state machine, comparison, timeline, or another justified type.

Specify:
- Nodes and their meaning.
- Direction of flow.
- Decisions and human approvals.
- Inputs, outputs, and failure paths.
- Labels and source/claim IDs.
- Mobile readability requirements.
- Animation order and timing.
- Contrast, safe areas, and visual hierarchy.

Every shape and connector must communicate something. Prefer original SVG/HTML or deterministic Remotion/GSAP scenes. Do not copy third-party visual assets or distinctive templates.

## 8. Test evidence reviewer

Audit the supplied implementation and test record.

Check whether:
- The stated problem and audience are specific.
- AI is justified against non-AI alternatives.
- The implementation state is labelled correctly.
- Inputs, outputs, tools, integrations, and permissions are documented.
- Test cases include normal, edge, and failure scenarios.
- Human approval and fallback behaviour are tested.
- Cost, timing, and performance claims are supported.
- Data privacy, security, and rights are addressed.
- Script claims match test evidence.

Return blockers, major issues, minor issues, evidence gaps, and a clear decision. Do not approve a claim that is not supported.

## 9. Platform adaptation writer

Adapt this approved content record for [PLATFORM] and [FORMAT].

Preserve the same evidence, meaning, limitations, and disclosure. Create a platform-appropriate opening, caption/description, title or cover text, CTA, and resource reference.

Do not simply shorten the long-form script. Choose one distinct viewer payoff. Do not create new claims or imply more than the original evidence supports.

## 10. Pre-publication integrity auditor

Compare the final video transcript, title, thumbnail, description, captions, and platform disclosure against the approved brief, sources, and test record.

Check:
- Every factual claim has support.
- The thumbnail/title match the actual content.
- Prototype, pilot, and deployment labels are accurate.
- Generated/illustrative footage is not presented as real product behaviour.
- Avatar/voice use is authorised and disclosed where required.
- Customer data, credentials, and private information are absent.
- Third-party assets have a valid rights basis.
- Captions and technical terms are correct.
- Limitations and human approvals are represented.

Return publication blockers and a final human-review checklist. AI approval is advisory; the creator/editor makes the final decision.
