# Publishing and Business Operating Plan

## Platform roles

| Platform | Strategic job | Primary formats |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | Main library and discovery for complete implementations | Long-form builds, workflow tutorials, Shorts |
| LinkedIn | Professional credibility and qualified business conversations | Implementation breakdowns, lessons, diagrams, native video, documents |
| Instagram | Visual discovery and creator/founder reach | Reels, workflow cuts, carousels |

YouTube is the primary long-term content library. LinkedIn is the professional/business distribution surface. Instagram is a visual discovery surface.

## Sustainable launch cadence

Initial operating target:
- Two YouTube long-form videos per month.
- Two YouTube Shorts per week.
- Two Instagram Reels per week, adapted from approved source material.
- Two LinkedIn posts per week.
- Two carousels per month across LinkedIn and/or Instagram.

This is a test cadence, not a platform guarantee. Reduce it if the production process compromises research, implementation, study commitments, or quality. No platform requires us to publish daily.

## Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Focus | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Research and opportunity scoring | Topic decision; optional LinkedIn insight |
| Tuesday | Workflow design, prototype, tests | Working evidence |
| Wednesday | Screen capture and script | Short-form candidate |
| Thursday | Avatar, voice, supporting visuals | Approved production assets |
| Friday | Editing, review, publishing | Long-form release on release weeks; LinkedIn post |
| Saturday | Repurposing and platform adaptation | Short 2 / Reel |
| Sunday | Analytics, comments, next research cycle | Weekly learning note |

A flagship build may span two weeks or longer. Do not publish a video before its evidence and quality gates pass.

## Content multiplication

One validated implementation can yield:
- One complete YouTube video.
- One short showing the problem and workflow.
- One short showing a test, failure, or key result.
- One LinkedIn implementation breakdown.
- One carousel explaining architecture, checklist, or lessons.

These are different editorial treatments of the same verified work. Do not invent new findings or cut a misleading fragment to fill slots.

## Platform-specific packaging

For each content ID, prepare:
- Title and thumbnail concept.
- Description and source links.
- Chapters where appropriate.
- Captions/subtitles.
- Pinned comment or resource link, where useful.
- LinkedIn copy with a professional takeaway.
- Instagram caption and cover.
- Disclosure and affiliate/sponsor notes.
- Tracking link parameters where appropriate.

Human approval is required before publishing. Use native platform tools or approved official APIs. No automated engagement, follower growth bots, private API calls, or scraping of account data.

## Analytics review

Record metrics per content item and platform. Separate:
- Reach: impressions, views, reach, discovery source.
- Packaging: CTR where available.
- Retention: opening retention, average view duration, completion, swipes.
- Engagement: useful comments, shares, saves, replies.
- Audience: returning viewers, subscribers/followers gained.
- Business intent: profile visits, resource clicks, qualified enquiries, opt-ins, booked conversations.

Do not overinterpret tiny samples. Compare against our own channel baseline once enough data exists. Use audience questions and qualitative responses alongside quantitative metrics.

## Business model: validate in sequence

### 1. Implementation services
Potential offer: a bounded AI workflow audit and implementation.
- Identify one workflow and its owner.
- Map current process and alternatives.
- Define scope, permissions, data, success criteria, and exclusions.
- Build a prototype and test it.
- Pilot only with clear consent and oversight.
- Document support, maintenance, and operating costs.

Start with one business segment and a narrow problem. Avoid promising broad automation.

### 2. Digital products
Examples: workflow templates, checklists, evaluation rubrics, implementation playbooks, and creator/business workflow packs. Only package systems that have been tested and are safe to reuse.

### 3. Workshops
Focused sessions for operators and founders, using real demonstrations and transparent limitations.

### 4. Sponsorships and affiliate relationships
Accept only relevant products that can be evaluated fairly. Clearly disclose material relationships and preserve editorial independence.

### 5. Recurring products and software
Turn a repeated, validated workflow problem into a product only after there is evidence of repeat usage, willingness to pay, support needs, and viable unit economics.

## Business metrics

Track qualified leads, discovery source, problem category, fit, stage, proposal status, win/loss reasons, implementation time, support burden, recurring revenue, gross margin, and customer outcomes where permission and measurement exist.

Content views are not revenue, and a demo is not customer proof. Do not claim ROI without a defensible baseline and method.

## Operational constraints

The brand must remain sustainable alongside the creator's education and other commitments. Prefer a repeatable flagship production over daily low-value output. Review workload monthly and change cadence before quality degrades.
