# 30-Day Launch Experiment

## Objective

Validate the AI That Works positioning, production workflow, avatar quality, audience response, and early business interest. The first month is an experiment, not a promise of follower or revenue growth.

## Hypotheses

1. Practical end-to-end workflows create stronger audience value than generic AI tool announcements.
2. Business operations and creator workflows attract distinct but relevant audiences.
3. An AI-avatar-led presenter can work when the majority of the proof comes from actual screens, systems, and test results.
4. Honest comparisons and visible limitations help build trust.
5. A sustainable workflow can be maintained alongside other commitments.

## Before Day 1

- Confirm brand name/handles and visual identity availability.
- Finalise avatar and voice test.
- Prepare OBS and Resolve templates.
- Configure Drive folders and content IDs.
- Create the research/competitor/opportunity tracker.
- Prepare the platform channels and disclosure settings.
- Set up analytics baseline and source links.
- Choose synthetic test data for the first demonstrations.

## Week 1 — Foundation and topic validation

- Validate the avatar with at least three short passages and multiple shots.
- Score identity, voice, lip sync, delivery, visual integrity, repeatability, and cost.
- Prepare a consistent studio/presenter look.
- Configure Google-first research and knowledge folders.
- Research 20 problem-led video opportunities.
- Audit existing videos for the strongest candidate topics.
- Score opportunities and choose the first two implementation tests.

**Exit criteria:** a repeatable production setup, a scored backlog, two defined problems, and a documented avatar decision.

## Week 2 — First implementation

- Map the current workflow and its non-AI alternatives.
- Build a bounded enquiry-management prototype using synthetic data.
- Test normal, ambiguous, duplicate, missing-data, and failure cases.
- Record inputs, outputs, retries, human approvals, time, and actual costs where available.
- Write the script only after evidence is ready.
- Produce one long-form video and two short-form derivatives.
- Publish a LinkedIn implementation note.

**Exit criteria:** the final video accurately represents the tested prototype and passes quality review.

## Week 3 — Second implementation

- Build a document assistant using a defined and approved document set.
- Test grounded answers, missing evidence, conflicting sources, access boundaries, and escalation.
- Capture real screens and test outputs.
- Produce the second flagship video or continue production if the evidence requires more time.
- Publish two short-form pieces and two LinkedIn updates.
- Collect recurring audience questions.

**Exit criteria:** a second useful demonstration with its own evidence record.

## Week 4 — Evaluation and optimisation

- Publish remaining planned short-form derivatives only if they add distinct value.
- Publish two educational/professional carousels where useful.
- Review platform analytics without overinterpreting small samples.
- Compare topic, hook, audience, retention, meaningful comments, saves/shares, and profile/resource clicks.
- Collect feedback from actual workflow owners where possible.
- Re-score the backlog using first-party evidence.
- Review workload, production cost, and sustainability.
- Decide what to repeat, revise, or stop next month.

**Exit criteria:** a written experiment report and a prioritised next-month backlog.

## Initial 30-day output targets

| Output | Target |
|---|---:|
| Research-backed topic ideas | 20 |
| Tested implementations | 2 |
| YouTube long-form videos | 2 |
| YouTube Shorts | 8 |
| Instagram Reels | 8 |
| LinkedIn posts | 8 |
| Carousels | 2 |
| Weekly learning reviews | 4 |

Targets are planning guides. Do not publish low-quality work just to hit a number. Cross-posting may use the same clean master with platform-specific framing, metadata, and captions.

## Evaluation scorecard

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Demand | Were problems supported by multiple independent signals? |
| Audience fit | Did the intended audience respond? |
| Packaging | Did title, thumbnail, and hook attract the right viewers? |
| Retention | Where did viewers leave, replay, or engage? |
| Usefulness | Did comments ask practical follow-up questions or describe real use? |
| Trust | Did viewers understand what was demoed versus deployed? |
| Production | How long did research, build, scripting, avatar, editing, and QA take? |
| Cost | What were tool, API, media, and human-time costs? |
| Business | Were there qualified requests, resource clicks, or discovery conversations? |
| Sustainability | Could this cadence continue without harming studies, projects, or quality? |

## Decision rules

- Repeat formats that outperform our own baseline and generate useful audience response.
- Improve topics that have demand but weak packaging or confusing demonstrations.
- Stop formats that attract irrelevant views but no meaningful engagement after a fair sample.
- Do not infer a permanent audience preference from one video.
- Change one major variable at a time where possible.
- Record sample size, date range, and platform context for every conclusion.

## End-of-experiment report

Document:
- What we published and when.
- Topic scores before publication.
- Performance by platform.
- Retention and engagement observations.
- Audience questions and qualitative feedback.
- Avatar/voice quality findings.
- Actual production time and costs.
- Implementation failures and corrections.
- Business inquiries and what they requested.
- What to continue, change, stop, and test next.
